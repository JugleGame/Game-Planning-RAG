## Components
- Core rule: the core is placing and upgrading defensive towers along a path to stop waves of enemies coming down a fixed route
  [source: MasterClass "Tower Defense Game Genre", verified 2026-07].
- ELEM-004 (repetition mechanic) - the repetition structure in which rounds continue and increasingly strong enemies arrive is the genre's basic skeleton [interpretation].
- ELEM-018 (roguelike random draft) - in the recent sub-cluster (roguelike tower defense) it is the key axis that produces a different tower
  combination every run, but it is not the majority of the genre as a whole [interpretation].
- ELEM-042 (single-tower direct-control defense) - as GAME-050 (Towerful Defense) demonstrates, unlike the orthodox approach of placing
  many towers, a sub-branch that directly controls a single tower with skills is also appearing [interpretation].
- ELEM-045 (deity/patron-selection summon roster lock) - the Greek-mythology roguelite TD 'Gods & Gore' uses a structure in which you pick
  a god and afterwards may summon only creatures belonging to that god, and it is observed as a concrete lock
  mechanic candidate for the subject-matter concentration trend above [source: ELEM-045 card].

## Market Saturation
- The work counted as the first tower defense is Rampart in 1990, and after passing through the Warcraft III map editor in the early 2000s
  it was popularized by Plants vs. Zombies in 2009 [source: MasterClass / Wellgames synthesis, verified 2026-07].
- The orthodox representative work GAME-026 (Bloons TD 6) has 97% positive out of 390,000 plus 1,317 Steam reviews alone, with estimated revenue
  of $73,700,000 to $93,000,000 [source: games-stats.com/Raijin tally, verified 2026-07].
- The roguelike sub-cluster's representative work GAME-027 (Rogue Tower) has 80% positive out of 4,356 reviews, a smaller sample
  than the orthodox side but forming a separate fanbase [source: review aggregation site, verified 2026-07].

## Conventions and Expectations
- Users expect fixed or semi-fixed routes, wave-by-wave difficulty escalation, and tower upgrades and elemental matchups as defaults
  [source: MasterClass "Tower Defense Game Genre", verified 2026-07].
- [interpretation] Recently, as sub-genres mixing in roguelike elements (random drafts, run-based unlocks) increase, the past expectation that
  "there is one same optimal solution every run" is being shaken.

## Gaps

was confirmed during the investigation. Given that the negative cases of GAME-027 (Rogue Tower) produced the reaction that "a random
path where you cannot see the next tile decides the outcome, which feels unfair", laying a pity rule of "a good
path/card is guaranteed to appear within N runs" onto the draft leaves room to reduce this complaint while keeping the fun peculiar
to roguelikes.
