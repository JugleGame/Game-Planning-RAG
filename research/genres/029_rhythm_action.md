## Components
- Beat-synchronized input - buttons, movement, and attacks must be timed to a note chart or soundtrack, while mistimed inputs receive a worse judgment or fail [source: composite of genre definitions, verified 2026]. Crypt of the NecroDancer pushes this element to an extreme by quantising movement, attacks, and spellcasting to the beat, so missing the timing costs the entire turn [source: genre overview reporting (AVClub-affiliated), verified 2026].
- ELEM-031 (exaggerated visual feedback) - screen shake, particles, and punchy impact sounds react immediately whenever input matches the beat, making success itself feel like a reward. Hi-Fi Rush (Tango Gameworks) is cited as a representative example because every attack, dodge, and movement is synchronized to the beat [source: FinalBoss.io reporting, verified 2026].
- Input-device and mode diversification - beyond traditional button input, motion controllers (Beat Saber), dance pads, touchscreens, and other input methods divide the genre into sub-branches [source: composite of genre overviews, verified 2026].

## Market Saturation
- Fact: since the mid-2020s, many new rhythm games have come from indie developers, while major-studio participation such as Tango Gameworks' Hi-Fi Rush has been reported as exceptional [source: AVClub "Rhythm games hit a new high note thanks to independent developers", verified 2026].
- [interpretation] The narrative that "the genre died and came back" repeats - multiple reports commonly diagnose hybrids that place rhythm elements on other genres such as roguelikes and action games as the force behind the revival after traditional music games such as Guitar Hero declined.

## Conventions and Expectations
- Fact: the convention is divided between traditional note-chart rhythm games such as osu! and Taiko no Tatsujin modes, and hybrids that place rhythm on another genre's rules (Crypt of the NecroDancer = roguelike, Hi-Fi Rush = action, Thumper = rail-shooter-like) [source: composite of genre overviews, verified 2026].
- [interpretation] Pure note-chart games follow mature conventions such as osu!, Taiko, Mania, and Catch, while most recent high-profile releases have been hybrids of "rhythm + an existing genre" - a signal that traditional rhythm games alone have difficulty creating new attention.
