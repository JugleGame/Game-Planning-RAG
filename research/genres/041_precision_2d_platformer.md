## Components

- Authored, non-generated stages traversed from the side, where the layout itself is the content and the same layout is replayed until it is beaten. [interpretation]
- A small movement verb set — run, jump, and usually one signature verb such as a dash, grapple, or wall interaction — kept constant for the whole game so that stages, not abilities, supply the variety. [interpretation]
- Stage-level teaching, since a fixed verb set can only stay interesting if the arrangement keeps posing new questions: ELEM-053 (Four-beat Stage Structure) and ELEM-054 (Wordless Onboarding Stage) are the two authoring patterns this cluster runs on. [interpretation]
- A retry loop as an explicit design object rather than a failure state: ELEM-055 (Bite-sized Rooms with Instant Respawn) makes the cost of a death a few seconds, which is what allows the precision demand in the first place. [interpretation]
- Accessibility as a shipped feature rather than a difficulty slider, covered by ELEM-052 (Assist and Accessibility Options), because the cluster's core appeal excludes players by execution ability rather than by taste. [interpretation]
- Adjacent but distinct clusters: GENRE-017 (Metroidvania) shares the side-view and the movement verbs but replaces authored linear stages with non-linear reopening, and GENRE-014 (Side-scrolling Horror) shares the camera but makes evasion and hiding the verbs instead of execution. [interpretation]

## Market Saturation

- Supply is high and still rising: 2D platformers were being released at roughly "150 per month" toward the end of 2024, a rate that tracks Steam's overall growth rather than exceeding it [source: Game Oracle, "Can 2D Platformers Still Be Profitable on Steam?", Ross Burton, as of 2025-07-11].
- The median outcome is poor in absolute terms and worse than the platform baseline: a median 2D platformer sells about 200 copies, against about 375 for all other genres, while initial pricing differs by only about one dollar [source: Game Oracle, "Can 2D Platformers Still Be Profitable on Steam?", Ross Burton, as of 2025-07-11].
- The gap holds at the threshold level. 18.1% of indie 2D platformers reach 1,000 or more sales, against 29.6% of indie titles across all genres; publisher-backed 2D platformers reach it at 44.7% [source: Game Oracle, "Can 2D Platformers Still Be Profitable on Steam?", Ross Burton, as of 2025-07-11].
- The shortfall is not a quality signal. Median review score for 2D platformers is 69.1%, against a 68.1% average for other genres, so well-reviewed releases are still landing under the platform's sales median [source: Game Oracle, "Can 2D Platformers Still Be Profitable on Steam?", Ross Burton, as of 2025-07-11].
- The same analysis stops short of calling the cluster closed, stating that opportunities remain in less-crowded segments and that differentiation has to come from mechanics or art rather than from executing the standard form well [source: Game Oracle, "Can 2D Platformers Still Be Profitable on Steam?", Ross Burton, as of 2025-07-11].
- [interpretation] Read together, these figures describe a visibility problem rather than a demand problem: the review data says buyers who arrive are satisfied, and the sales data says too few arrive. Budgeting a project in this cluster on the median is the only defensible default.

## Conventions and Expectations

- Death is cheap and instant. The convention was set explicitly: "It was very important that the levels in Super Meat Boy be bite-sized", and "The time it takes for Meat Boy to die and respawn is almost instantaneous. The player never waits to get back into the game, the pace never drops" [source: Game Developer, "Super Meat Boy's McMillen Explains 'Why So Hard?'", Edmund McMillen, as of 2010-04-21].
- Lives are gone. The penalty for death is expected to be "the amount of time you took to restart after death and the length of the current level", not a lost run or a lost resource [source: Game Developer, "Super Meat Boy's McMillen Explains 'Why So Hard?'", Edmund McMillen, as of 2010-04-21].
- Stages are expected to teach without stopping play. The reference point is World 1-1 of Super Mario Bros., built so the player would "gradually and naturally understand what they're doing" and then "start to play more freely" [source: Eurogamer interview with Shigeru Miyamoto, as of 2015-09-08].
- Stages are expected to be about something. The stated authoring order is introduce, develop, change up, conclude, with the mechanic retired afterwards [source: Game Developer, "The secret to Mario level design", Christian Nutt, as of 2012-04-13].
- Room-level design vocabulary is public and discussed as craft, not as internal process — the GDC 2018 Celeste level-design workshop presents rooms as the teaching unit and cheap, informative failure as part of the grammar [source: GDC 2018, "Level Design Workshop: Designing Celeste", Maddy Thorson, listing as of 2026-08-22].
- [interpretation] The practical expectation this creates: a buyer in this cluster judges the movement feel and the first stage within minutes, because both are conventions they already know how to evaluate.
