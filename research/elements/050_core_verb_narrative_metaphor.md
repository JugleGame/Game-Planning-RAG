## Definition
The button the player uses most is also part of the story's meaning. Swinging can express who the hero is. Blinking can mean losing time. Running can mean refusing to face a memory. The ending changes that familiar action instead of adding an unrelated final mini-game.

## Success Cases
- GAME-057 (SANABI): a signature chain-hook arm is both the central traversal/combat tool and the protagonist's defining physical feature; the game won Best Action Adventure Game. [source: Steam, as of 2026-08-13] [source: NEOWIZ, as of 2024-07-31]
- GAME-059 (Before Your Eyes): blinking advances memories even when the player wants to remain, directly embodying time and loss; it won the BAFTA Game Beyond Entertainment award. [source: GoodbyeWorld Games developer FAQ, as of 2021-04-08] [source: BAFTA, as of 2022-04-07]

## Failure Cases
- GAME-060 (SOMA): Frictional stated that monster-chase gameplay was not the core experience, yet Amnesia-led marketing encouraged conventional scare expectations. [source: Frictional Games, SOMA One Year Later, as of 2016-09-22]
- [interpretation] This is the failure shape: the advertised verb and the story's real emotional verb point in different directions.

## User Reaction Summary
- GAME-057 and GAME-059 hold Overwhelmingly Positive Steam reception, and official award descriptions recognize their action or beyond-entertainment experience. [source: Steam, as of 2026-08-13] [source: NEOWIZ, as of 2024-07-31] [source: BAFTA, as of 2022-04-07]
- [interpretation] Players reject the device when the unusual input is unreliable or when the metaphor is clear but the action is not enjoyable.

## Risks
- [interpretation] A symbolic mechanic still needs responsive controls; meaning does not excuse friction.
- [interpretation] Do not disable a learned action without readable feedback, or the climax looks broken instead of intentional.
- [interpretation] Reversal requires prior hints and a safe retry path so experimentation is possible without arbitrary punishment.
