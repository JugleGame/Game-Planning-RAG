## Definition
The game first lets you do normal things with someone: eat, walk, play, talk, or remember home. Later, frightening scenes reuse those places, sounds, or habits. The scary part matters more because the player already knows what “safe” felt like.

## Success Cases
- GAME-058 (OMORI): its official description joins colorful friends and mundane spaces to the recovery of a forgotten past; the game sold 1,000,000 copies and holds 97% positive English Steam sentiment. [source: OMOCAT official blog, as of 2022-12-31] [source: Steam, as of 2026-08-13]
- GAME-059 (Before Your Eyes): family and first-love memories sit beside illness, grief, and repressed truth; it won the BAFTA Game Beyond Entertainment award. [source: Steam, as of 2026-08-13] [source: BAFTA, as of 2022-04-07]

## Failure Cases
- GAME-060 (SOMA) is a boundary warning: Frictional reported that monster-chase expectations could overshadow the intended first-person philosophical experience. [source: Frictional Games, SOMA One Year Later, as of 2016-09-22]
- [interpretation] If safe activities are only a short tutorial, the player recognizes setup machinery instead of forming a bond.

## User Reaction Summary
- Players have sustained Overwhelmingly Positive Steam reception for both GAME-058 and GAME-059, supporting demand for emotional material beside darker revelation. [source: Steam, as of 2026-08-13]
- [interpretation] The likely dislike point is tonal manipulation: warmth feels dishonest when every pleasant scene exists only to be destroyed.

- ELEM-050 (Core Verb as Narrative Metaphor): good fit — the same activity can feel playful by day and threatening in a nightmare.
- ARCH-032 (Narrative Beat and Flag Ledger): required for spacing safe beats, anomaly beats, and horror beats without accidental repetition.

## Risks
- [interpretation] Repeating low-interaction bonding scenes can make the opening feel like delayed gameplay.
- [interpretation] Horror must transform a specific shared detail; generic monsters do not pay off the relationship work.
- [interpretation] Content warnings are necessary when grief, illness, suicide, or trauma are central rather than incidental.
