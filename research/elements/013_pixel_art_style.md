## Definition
Pixel art draws images by placing small square pixels one by one. It may resemble old arcade games, but indie developers use it because characters and worlds can be made quickly with small teams and budgets.

## Success Cases
- GAME-018 (Terraria) - Chose an XNA-based 2D pipeline and pixel art, increasing development speed without 3D modeling or animation, and later reached 70,000,000 cumulative copies [source: GAME-018 card].
- GAME-019 (Stardew Valley) - A solo developer learned pixel art and produced the game for four years, reaching one of Steam’s highest ratings [source: GAME-019 card].
- GAME-020 (Core Keeper) - A recent case showing that changing the viewpoint while inheriting Terraria’s grammar can still produce a separate hit [source: GAME-020 card].

## User Reaction Summary
- Like: Retro nostalgia, low hardware requirements, and the defense that “bad pixels are better than bad 3D” [source: ResetEra thread/re-cited by game-oracle.com, as of 2026].
- Dislike: “Everything looks the same (fat-pixel),” criticism that it looks like cost cutting rather than an artistic choice, and reactions that pixel art in 2D indie games is “getting tiring” [source: ResetEra thread (“The never ending trend of 2D indie games going after the pixel art look is getting tiring”), re-cited, as of 2026].
