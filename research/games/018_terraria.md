## Summary and Sales/Review Metrics
Re-Logic's 2D pixel-art sandbox released in 2011. Cumulative sales of 70,000,000 units at the 15th-anniversary mark (PC 39,600,000 + console 10,700,000 + mobile 19,700,000) [source: ingamenews.com/verticalslicegames.com, as of 2026-05]. Of more than 1,000,000 Steam reviews, 97% are positive ("Overwhelmingly Positive") [source: GamesRadar/Dualshockers (citing Steam data), verified 2026-07].

## Elements Used
- ELEM-013 (Pixel-Art Style) - Adopted 2D pixel art based on Microsoft XNA, raising development speed without a 3D modeling and animation pipeline [source: re-cited from an article summarizing Re-Logic's development background (allkeyshop.com)].

## Success/Failure Drivers
- Developer Andrew Spinks said he was inspired by Minecraft but felt it "lacked structured purpose and progression," so he combined the sandbox's freedom with Metroidvania-style progression (unlocking new areas with equipment) [source: re-cited from an article summarizing an Andrew Spinks interview (bysolopreneurs.com)].
- [interpretation] It appears that after the interlocking "crafting-exploration-survival" loop gained exposure through the early comparison to "2D Minecraft," it succeeded in differentiating itself with its own content such as boss fights, biomes, and events.

## Implications for Our Project
[interpretation] Evidence that open-world-style motivation is possible without a 3D pipeline, using only rule-based progression (equipment unlock → access to new areas) - the core strategy of a low-cost 2D pipeline and the GENRE-006 cluster.
