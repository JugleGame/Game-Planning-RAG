## Summary and Sales/Review Metrics
The Zelda series’ transition to open world on Wii U/Switch. 34,320,000 cumulative copies sold [source: Statista (Nintendo performance aggregate), as of 2025-03]. Metacritic 98 (63 reviews) [source: Metacritic, 2026-07 check].

## Elements Used
- ELEM-011 (emergent system interaction) - The team created elemental rules for fire, ice, wind, and more, calling them a “chemistry engine,” and designed objects to react to one another [source: GDC 2017 presentation (Takuhiro Dohta·Hidemaro Fujibayashi), quoted in a Thumbsticks summary article].
- ELEM-012 (landmark-based exploration) - Having players see and walk to landmarks (towers, mountains, shrines) to verify them reduces dependence on quest markers [interpretation].

## Success/Failure Drivers
- The team described this as a shift from “additive design” to “multiplicative design”—objects were designed to react not only to player actions but also to one another [source: quoted GDC 2017 presentation, Thumbsticks summary article].
- [interpretation] Immersion comes from using real-world physical laws (rocks roll downhill, lightning travels through metal, wind pushes objects) directly as rules, allowing players to solve puzzles intuitively.

## Implications for Our Project
[interpretation] Even without placing content manually one piece at a time, a few well-designed interaction rules can let players discover combinations themselves and increase replayability—there is strong potential to combine this with AI-based response design (ELEM-005).
