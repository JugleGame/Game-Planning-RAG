## Summary and Sales/Review Metrics
An indie roguelike deckbuilder revealed in early access in late 2017 and formally released in January 2019 [source: GameShub genre
analysis article, verified 2026-07]. It has passed 10,000,000 units across PC and console combined [source: Alinea Analytics, as of 2026-03-20].
97% positive out of more than 194,000 Steam user reviews [source: Notebookcheck sale coverage, verified 2026-07].
The sequel Slay the Spire 2 launched on 5 March 2026 in early access at $25 and in two weeks recorded more than 4,600,000 units / $92,000,000,
and over the 14-15 March weekend left a peak DAU of 2,200,000 and 94% positive reviews [source: Alinea Analytics,
as of 2026-03-20].

## Elements Used
- ELEM-020 (Deck Building) - a structure where you start from a weak starting deck and add one card at the end of each battle to
  complete a deck of your own [source: Eneba review article, verified 2026-07].
- ELEM-018 (Roguelike Random Upgrade/Path Draft) - each run differs because of the order of challenges on the map, the choice among
  randomly offered card rewards, and unpredictable relic acquisition [source: Eneba review article,
  verified 2026-07].

## Success/Failure Drivers
- Fact: Each run is generated from a unique seed, and the Daily Climb mode shares this seed across all players so that map layout,
  card rewards, relic drops and event outcomes all come out identical [source: Slay the Spire Wiki 'Daily Climb'
  page, verified 2026-07].
- [interpretation] The key is that seed sharing removes the chronic complaint of random games, "they just got lucky." If results differ despite
  receiving the same hand, the difference can only be explained by skill, so you can build a competitive framing while keeping randomness.
- Fact: Each time you finish a run the next Ascension difficulty unlocks, and each tier adds one concrete modification such as stronger enemies,
  more elites, fewer potion slots or higher shop prices [source: Eneba review article, verified 2026-07].
- [interpretation] Because clearing is not the end but the key that opens the next lock, it extends playtime without building new content. It is an
  especially cost-efficient approach at indie scale.
- Fact: In the sequel, more than 50% of players passed 20 hours, 14% passed 50 hours and 1% passed 100 hours
  [source: Alinea Analytics, as of 2026-03-20].
- Fact: The sequel's two-week revenue already exceeded the cumulative Steam revenue of Hollow Knight: Silksong ($83,000,000) and Hades II
  ($82,000,000) [source: Alinea Analytics, as of 2026-03-20].

## Implications for Our Project
If you want to use randomness while avoiding the "blame it on luck" controversy, **making players share the same seed** is cheaper and more
certain than tinkering with probabilities [interpretation]. Also, unlocking difficulty in stages the way the Ascension structure does is an indie
solution for extending playtime without new content [interpretation]. It is exactly the counterpoint to the "unfair gamble" complaint in
GAME-027 (Rogue Tower).
