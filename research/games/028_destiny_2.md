## Summary and Sales/Review Metrics
At launch, Destiny 2 designed weapon perks as fixed (static rolls), so anyone who obtained a weapon with the same name got the same perks [source: GameRant 'Destiny 2 Weapons Won't Have Random Perk Rolls', as of 2017].
Random rolls returned with the Forsaken expansion and 2.0 update on 2018-09-04 [source: Newsweek 'Destiny 2
Stream Recap - Random Rolls, Mods & Sandbox Tweaks Detailed', as of 2018-08-07].
After Forsaken launched, the player count tripled—on August 28 it was approximately 462,000 PvP / 767,000 PvE players [source: PC Games Insider, as of 2018]. According to SuperData, more than 60% of monthly active players purchased Forsaken [source: PCGamesN, as of 2018-11-08].

## Elements Used
- ELEM-019 (random loot drops & loot table) - Even the same weapon drops with a different perk combination each time, making players check “what roll is this one?” when an owned weapon drops again [source: Newsweek, as of 2018-08-07].
  This is supplemented by masterwork (upgradable to 10 levels, increasing a specific stat by 10%) and random armor stat rolls [source: Newsweek, as of 2018-08-07].

## Success/Failure Drivers
- Fact: In the first year with fixed rolls, players said there were only a few weapons they truly wanted, and after collecting them one by one there was no reason to keep farming [source: aggregate Bungie.net forum discussions, as of 2018].
- Fact: After random rolls returned, duplicate drops made players open their inventories to check the roll, and a good roll prompted them to brag in party chat [source: Kotaku 'Help, I'm Drowning In Destiny 2 Gear', as of 2018].
- [interpretation] Repeated drops appear to have extended farming’s life because “the contents are different every time.” Fixed rolls have a finite collection list that ends when completed, while random rolls keep the goal open.
- Fact: Player counts and monthly active users increased, but Activision said Forsaken was “not performing as well as we'd like” [source: PCGamesN, as of 2018-11-08]. During the same period, Destiny 2 topped the September PS4·Xbox One sales chart ahead of Spider-Man, FIFA 19, and Fortnite [source: PCGamesN, as of 2018-11-08].
  Failure point: engagement recovered, but did not translate into returning purchases from lapsed core users, so the metrics and revenue diverged.
- Fact: Complaints about randomness continued afterward, with repeated complaints about low drop rates for specific exotics and lost sectors and desired rolls never appearing—a case of one player completing 978 Trials matches without getting the target roll became a community talking point [source: Sportskeeda report on community reactions, 2026-07 check].
  Failure point: Random rolls without an upper bound become the feeling that “my time is not respected.”

## Implications for Our Project
Removing random rolls makes the collection finite and exhausts farming motivation early; adding them keeps the goal open and creates retention, but brings the complaint that “you may never get it” [interpretation]. The lesson of this card is not to choose one or the other, but to enable random rolls while adding an upper bound such as ELEM-017’s pity concept. This case also shows that we must not assume metrics (player count) and revenue move together [interpretation].
