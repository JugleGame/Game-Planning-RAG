## Summary and Sales/Review Metrics
SOMA is a first-person sci-fi horror story about identity, consciousness, and what it means to be human. Steam lists 46,221 purchaser reviews across all languages as Overwhelmingly Positive and 24,264 English reviews as Overwhelmingly Positive. [source: Steam, as of 2026-08-13]

## Elements Used
- ELEM-049 (Suppressed Memory and Identity Reconstruction): locked terminals, documents, and encounters are used to uncover the truth behind the station and the protagonist's situation. [source: Steam SOMA store description, as of 2026-08-13]
- ELEM-050 (Core Verb as Narrative Metaphor): cautionary use — running and hiding are prominent verbs, but Frictional later stated that monster-chase play was not the core SOMA experience. [source: Frictional Games, SOMA One Year Later, as of 2016-09-22]

## Success/Failure Drivers
- Ten days after launch, Frictional reported a Metacritic score of 85 and 94% positive Steam reviews, with reviewers especially praising the thought-provoking narrative. [source: Frictional Games, SOMA Ten Days After Launch, as of 2015-10-01]
- The developer reported that marketing through its Amnesia heritage produced expectations of a more conventional scare-focused sequel, even though SOMA's intended core was first-person philosophical disturbance. [source: Frictional Games, SOMA One Year Later, as of 2016-09-22]
- Current Steam reception remains Overwhelmingly Positive across 46,221 purchaser reviews. [source: Steam, as of 2026-08-13]

## Implications for Our Project
- [interpretation] Do not let chase footage define the promise if the real payoff is grief and acceptance; the store-facing pitch must communicate the emotional investigation.
- [interpretation] Every monster should represent or guard a specific memory pressure, rather than functioning as interchangeable pursuit content.
- [interpretation] If the climax asks the player to stop running, earlier encounters need consistent evidence that the threat may be misunderstood.
